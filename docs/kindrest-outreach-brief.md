## What Kindrest is

A daily companion app for mothers, live and free at **kindrest.co**.

It opens with **one question a day** — the same question for everyone, answerable
in about fifteen seconds. She writes a sentence, or taps "not today." If she taps
not-today, it asks one thing: *is today quiet, or is it heavy?* Heavy means it
stops asking for a bright side and puts real support in front of her instead.

From there she can go deeper if she wants:
- **Check-in** — two minutes on mood, mind, body and available time, returning a
  few micro-practices sized to what she actually has
- **Rest Card** — a 3×3 grid of small things that might already be true
  ("you drank something warm"). She taps what's true. Completing a line is a
  celebration, then a fresh card
- **Journal**, and a history of her own patterns
- **Her village** — a private link she sends to people who love her, so they can
  leave her a note. It appears on her home screen

**Design principles, which are non-negotiable and shape all copy:**
- No streaks, no scores, no guilt. A hard day is not a missed day
- She never has to *earn* anything. The founding line is *"You don't have to earn
  your rest"*
- No toxic positivity. It never asks her to perform being okay
- Crisis language in anything she writes surfaces real resources immediately

## Who it's for

Mothers in the **0–3 year window** — after the leave policy stops applying, when
the load is heaviest and least visible. Not pregnancy, not birth. The part
afterwards that nothing covers.

Founder is a Black woman, Spelman alum, Atlanta-based, solo, building from her
own experience. Early users skew Black and Atlanta-connected.

the *old* version — the current product only shipped on 6 August, and none of
them have seen it.

## Business model

- **B2C: free.** No paywall, no waitlist, no gate. A paid tier is scoped but
  deliberately not built — there's no retention to monetise yet
- **B2B: "Kindrest @ Work."** $5,000 flat for a 3-month pilot covering a
  50-person cohort. After the pilot: $350/mo up to 25 people, $650/mo up to 50.
  A smaller $1,200 / 15-seat SKU is planned but not yet on the pricing page

**The B2B pitch is utilization, not ROI.** With 19 users any ROI model would be
fiction and a sharp benefits buyer would dismantle it. The honest line is:
*"EAPs get 3–5% usage and everyone knows it. I'll show you utilization weekly and
we'll find out together."* Employers see enrollment and weekly-active counts only
— never anyone's check-ins or journal, which is architecturally true, not a
promise.

**Current pipeline:**
- **PagerDuty** — warm contact, in conversation, nothing signed
- **Atlanta Public Schools** — blocked on a notarized form; public-sector
  procurement means 6–18 months. Deliberately out of scope for this quarter
- **Season Marietta** — a Marietta breakfast spot, agreed in principle to put
  cards on the counter. First local partner

## The goal, and the constraint

**100 users and 25 weekly-active by 21 November 2026** (100 days from 13 August),
plus $10K in pilot revenue.

**Acquisition budget: $0–5 per user, ~$500 total.** No paid ads — maternal-health
CPMs put realistic CAC at $20–80, which is 4–16× the ceiling, and it would mean
buying churn before retention is proven.

**Everything is founder-led.** One person, no team, no agency, limited hours.
Assets must be things she can actually execute alone.

## What's already built to support outreach

These exist and work — plans should use them rather than invent new mechanics.

| Thing | What it does |
|---|---|
| **`kindrest.co/join/<name>`** | A branded page per partner — *"Kindrest × Season Marietta"*. Everyone who joins through it is attributed to that partner, so "twelve moms joined from your counter" is a real sentence |
| **One-tap invite** | Each user gets a share link; her friend lands on a page that greets her by the sender's first name |
| **Her village** | She shares a link, her people leave notes, no account needed for them |
| **Daily reminder email** | Sends every morning at 9am ET carrying that day's question |
| **Attribution tracking** | Captures UTM tags, referrer and device on arrival; signup asks "how did you hear about Kindrest?" So campaign performance is now measurable |
| **`/admin/report`** | Signups by week with activation and return rates, and landing-page conversion |

Help refine outreach and build the assets for it. Specifically:

3. **The physical card** for counters and vendor fairs — copy and design for

4. **Practitioner outreach** — doulas, midwives, postpartum OBs, pelvic floor

5. **A reactivation message** to the 11 people who used the old version and left,

6. **Vendor fair / event playbook** — what a table looks like, what converts
7. **Anything that makes word of mouth easier** — that's the only channel with a
   true $0 cost, and the invite and village features exist to support it

## Voice rules for anything written

- Plain, warm, unhurried. Short sentences. No exclamation marks
- Never imply she's failing, behind, or needs fixing
- Never "self-care" as a slogan, never "boss mom", never hustle framing
- Specific over clever. *"You drank something warm"* beats *"practice mindful
  hydration"*
- The product's own promise is small on purpose — fifteen seconds, one question.
  Don't oversell it into something bigger

## Hard nos

- **Don't invent numbers or outcomes.** No "join thousands of mothers." There are
  nineteen
- **Don't promise employers ROI**, retention percentages, or clinical outcomes
- **Don't recommend paid ads** at this stage
- **Don't design anything requiring a team**, a budget, or daily posting
  discipline she can't sustain
- **Don't add streaks, points, badges or leaderboards.** A points economy was
  built and deliberately deleted before launch
